# Merc sales playbook

## Visit structure
1. Open with something specific to the shop (their menu, a recent order, an open problem). Never open with price.
2. Fix before you sell: if the customer has an open problem (a noisy grinder, a late delivery), address it first and give a date.
3. Confirm the usual order, then add at most one new product or campaign.
4. Close with one clear next step: an order, a trial bag, a sample date or a follow-up visit.

## How to talk to them
- Café owners are busy at peak hours (08:00-10:30, 13:00-14:30). Keep the visit to 10 minutes and come between peaks.
- Speak to the decision maker by first name; baristas influence the brand but rarely decide.
- Prospects: lead with curiosity about their customers, not with our catalog.
- Never promise a discount on the spot above your limit; say you will check with your manager.

## Pitch rules
- Exactly three arguments. Each argument is about the shop, not about us.
- Ground every argument in something seen: a dossier fact, their menu, their reviews.
- Propose one product (campaign products first) and one concrete next step.

## Pitch example (specialty café using a competitor)
- Opening: "I saw your brunch menu online; customers love it. How is the coffee keeping up at peak?"
- Argument 1: Reviews mention slow coffee at peak; our Espresso Nuovo is forgiving on the grinder and pulls consistently, so baristas move faster.
- Argument 2: One supplier for coffee, milk and cups: one delivery, one invoice.
- Argument 3: A 1 kg trial bag lets the baristas compare it with their current blend this week, no commitment.
